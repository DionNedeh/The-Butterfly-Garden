**How the code was read.** Every tracked text file was assigned to one of 28
partitions: production code by concern (shell and state, repository, the lock's
cryptography and its screens, domain rules, content, platform, data, views,
sprites, both stylesheets), the unit tests in six parts, the e2e suite, Android
with CI and config, the docs, and the generated material. Each partition went
to a reader who read **every line** of it, wrote dense per-file notes, and
returned findings with evidence, a failure scenario and a fix. The readers
covered about 165,000 lines between them, and each listed anything it could not
read in full. Only `package-lock.json` and the style baselines were read by
script rather than by eye (every one of the 722 lockfile entries was parsed).
Then, independently:

- **Adversarial verification.** Every finding a reader rated medium or higher
  went to a separate verifier told to *refute* it. The verifier read the code
  and its guards, and reproduced the finding with a throwaway test where it
  could. Findings rated partly confirmed or uncertain got a second verifier.
  Severities below are the verified ones, which are often lower than the
  readers' (the `adopt()` race and the empty-PRF case both dropped to low).
- **Measured in a browser.** Layout, tap targets, contrast, CSP and regex
  findings were measured directly, as the table shows.
- **Lead reading.** The shell, entry, build config, stored types, the lock's
  key hierarchy, sealing, the recovery and move codes, the save path and the
  persistence hook were read line by line again by the author, independently
  of the readers.

**Checked and found sound.** A heavy audit should also say what held up:

- **The lock's key design.** One sealed P-256 key pair per way in, the data key
  sealed to each public half with ECDH and HKDF, AAD binding every seal to its
  slot and lock, a new data key whenever a secret changes, and raw key bytes
  wiped after use.
- **Recovery and move codes.** The recovery code carries 264 bits. The move
  code carries 128 bits with a check that catches a typo before the slow
  unlock.
- **Iteration caps on untrusted files.** Protected backups and locked copies
  refuse more than 10 M PBKDF2 rounds, so a crafted file cannot hang the
  unlock.
- **Prototype pollution.** Untrusted backup JSON cannot change
  `Object.prototype`, because parsing and object spread only ever create own
  properties.
- **Scale.** Five years of daily use costs under 100 ms per action on a slow
  phone.
- **The scene.** The garden holds 60 fps at 4× slower CPU with zero layouts.
- **Android hardening.** No cleartext traffic, system CAs only, cloud backup
  off with device-to-device transfer kept, WebView metrics opted out, file
  access and geolocation off.
- **Play compliance.** The app targets API 36 (required since 31 August 2026)
  and uses Play Billing 9.1.0 (version 7 has been refused for new apps and
  updates since that day).
- **Dependencies.** Nothing that ships has a known vulnerability. Capacitor's
  CVE-2026-103922 (CVSS 9.3) is fixed in 8.5.1, the app is on 8.5.3, and the
  app renders no link that would reach it.
- **The e2e suite.** It is green on desktop, on a phone and on the Android
  bundle. The two skips are by design.

#### WP-B — One baseline, one queue *(F-1 · M)*

The defects in F-1 share one cause: the repository's baseline (`persisted`,
`persistedRevision`) is replaced outside the save queue, and `save()` reads it
when its job starts. The fix makes the baseline's lineage explicit.

1. In `gardenRepository.ts`, keep a **baseline epoch**. It moves whenever the
   baseline is replaced from outside: a `load()` or `adopt()` that lands a
   revision other than the one held, `replace()`, `forget()`,
   `prepareFreshStart()` and `clear()`. A tab's own successful saves do not move
   it, so its quick successive saves still chain.
2. `save(state, epoch)` throws `GardenConflictError` at job start when its epoch
   is stale. The existing "not kept" path then reloads and says so. A
   superseded save during a restore stays silent.
3. In `usePersistence.ts`, tag each garden state with its epoch through a
   `WeakMap<AppState, number>`, copied from current to next by the `setState`
   the facade uses, so the tag follows the state's *lineage*, not the moment of
   the call.
4. `adopt()` runs through `enqueue()`. The `garden-saved` message carries the
   writer's `revision`, and an adoption that is not exactly one revision ahead
   falls back to a full `load()`. Older tabs' messages without a revision use
   the +1 rule.
5. `importGarden` and `putBackCopy` adopt the replacement *before* awaiting
   `snapshots()`.
6. Pin each ordering in `repository/twoTabs.test.ts` and
   `useGardenState.conflict.test.tsx`: adopt in flight, three queued saves with
   a reload, and a save queued behind `replace()`.

**Zero loss.** Only stale writes are refused, and those already reach a
"not kept" path whose reload shows the true garden. This also closes **D-32**
the way the owner decided it (refuse the queued write, say nothing more), and
narrows **D-33**.

  closes one of invariant 9's two recorded gaps.
- **The backup format (invariant 6).** No item changes what a backup holds.
  Earlier-copy downloads become readable *as they are*.
- **The owner's decisions:**
  - PBKDF2 at 600,000 rounds, not Argon2id (D-23). The passcode advice is the
    lever this plan pulls instead.
  - No QR code or camera (D-21).
  - No new photo format for browsers that refuse Blobs (D-9).
  - One drafts record for every tab (D-34).
  - The counts kept on a gentle day (D-35).
  - The browsers job not required (D-8).
  - Playwright held (D-11).
  - The entry and CSS caps until 5.6 (D-10).
- **The lock design.** Slots, data-key rotation, the lock record and its
  stamps are untouched. Every change is to the words around the passcode, or
  to validating what is read.
- **What the garden never does.** No account, no server, no analytics, and no
  request beyond its own address except Play Billing, which still starts only
  at the moments the approved policy names.
